# What is an Intelligent Agent?

### Overview
An intelligent agent is a sophisticated computer system designed to operate autonomously, perceive its environment, and act rationally to achieve specific goals. It distinguishes itself from basic programs by its ability to adapt, persist over time, and make decisions based on knowledge and inference.

### Key Concepts
*   **Agent:** A computer program or system that goes beyond simple execution, exhibiting autonomy, perception, persistence, adaptability, and goal-pursuit.
*   **Rational Agent:** An agent that consistently acts to achieve the best possible outcome, or the best expected outcome when uncertainty is present. This concept is central to building successful, intelligent systems.
*   **Autonomy:** The ability of an agent to operate independently without constant human intervention.
*   **Perception:** The capacity of an agent to sense and interpret information from its surrounding environment.
*   **Adaptation:** The ability of an agent to adjust its behavior or internal state in response to changes in its environment or new experiences.
*   **Goals:** Specific objectives or desired states that an agent is designed to achieve through its actions.
*   **Knowledge Base:** A structured repository within an agent where information about the world is stored, typically in a knowledge representation language.
*   **Inference Mechanism:** The component of a knowledge-based agent responsible for reasoning with the knowledge stored in the knowledge base to derive new conclusions or make decisions.
*   **Environment:** The context or setting in which an agent operates and interacts, such as the Internet.
*   **Performance Measure:** The criteria used to evaluate the success or rationality of an agent's actions within its environment.
*   **Sensors and Actuators:** Components that allow an agent to perceive its environment (sensors) and act upon it (actuators).

### Detailed Academic Explanation

An intelligent agent represents a fundamental concept in artificial intelligence, moving beyond the capabilities of conventional computer programs. While all computer programs perform some function, intelligent agents are characterized by a set of advanced attributes: they are expected to operate autonomously, perceiving their environment, persisting over a prolonged time period, adapting to change, and actively creating and pursuing goals. This distinction highlights their dynamic and self-directed nature, setting them apart from static, pre-programmed routines.

Central to the definition and design of an intelligent agent is the concept of a "rational agent." A rational agent is one that consistently acts in a manner calculated to achieve the best possible outcome in a given situation. In scenarios where uncertainty exists, a rational agent strives for the best *expected* outcome. This principle of rationality is considered foundational for developing successful agents—systems that can genuinely be described as intelligent. The application of rationality is broad, extending to a wide variety of agents operating in virtually any imaginable environment. The process of building such agents involves developing specific design principles rooted in this core concept of rational action.

To effectively make good decisions and act rationally, intelligent agents often require a sophisticated understanding of their operational context. This necessitates that intelligent agents possess knowledge about the world. This knowledge is not merely stored as raw data but is contained within the agent in the form of sentences expressed in a specific knowledge representation language, which are then organized and stored in a knowledge base. Consequently, many intelligent agents are structured as "knowledge-based agents," comprising both a knowledge base and an inference mechanism. The inference mechanism is crucial for processing this stored knowledge, allowing the agent to reason, draw conclusions, and ultimately inform its decision-making process. The emphasis on "correct inferences" in certain AI approaches further underscores the importance of robust reasoning capabilities within intelligent agents.

The operational success and rationality of an agent are not absolute but are contingent upon several factors. As illustrated by the example of a simple vacuum-cleaner agent, determining if an agent is rational depends critically on defining its performance measure, understanding what is known about its environment, and identifying the specific sensors it uses to perceive its surroundings and the actuators it employs to act upon them. These elements collectively shape an agent's ability to gather information, process it, and execute actions that align with its goals and the principle of rationality.

Intelligent agents are not merely theoretical constructs; they are increasingly prevalent in real-world applications. The Internet, for instance, serves as one of the most important environments for intelligent agents, where AI systems have become so common in web-based applications that the "-bot" suffix has entered everyday language. This widespread adoption signifies the practical utility and impact of intelligent agent technologies, which underlie many modern digital interactions. Architectures like SOAR are well-known examples of complete agent architectures, demonstrating the complex engineering involved in creating these sophisticated, autonomous systems. In essence, an intelligent agent is a dynamic, goal-oriented system that leverages perception, knowledge, and rational decision-making to operate effectively and adaptively within its environment.
